What Is an Aerial Survey?

An aerial survey uses an unmanned aircraft (drone or UAV) equipped with a precision sensor to capture geo-referenced data of the land surface from above. Unlike satellite imagery — which is limited by resolution, cloud cover, and update frequency — drone aerial surveys are performed on demand, at low altitude, and with centimeter-level ground resolution.

Two main sensor types are used in professional aerial surveying:

Photogrammetry (Camera-Based)

A high-resolution camera captures hundreds of overlapping photographs during a systematic flight pattern. Photogrammetry software processes these images into an orthophoto (a stitched, distortion-corrected aerial image) and a Digital Surface Model (DSM) — a dense 3D point cloud representing everything the camera saw, including vegetation, buildings, and terrain. Photogrammetry is ideal for open terrain, real estate photography, agricultural monitoring, and general topographic mapping where vegetation cover is not dense. See our photogrammetry service page for full equipment specs and deliverables.

LiDAR (Laser Scanning)

LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) fires laser pulses at the surface and measures the return time to calculate precise 3D coordinates. Because laser pulses can penetrate gaps in forest canopy, LiDAR produces a bare-earth Digital Terrain Model (DTM) even in densely vegetated areas where photogrammetry would only see the top of the tree canopy. LiDAR aerial surveys are the standard choice for forested land, steep terrain with ground cover, and high-precision construction applications. Visit our LiDAR mapping service page for equipment specifications and pricing, or read our LiDAR vs. photogrammetry comparison for a deeper breakdown.

Aerial Survey Applications in Costa Rica

Drone aerial surveys are used across virtually every industry that deals with land in Costa Rica. Here are the most common applications:

Real Estate & Property Development

Before purchasing land or beginning development, buyers and developers need accurate topographic data: boundary verification, slope analysis, drainage patterns, and existing vegetation mapping. A drone aerial survey delivers a complete site picture in a single flight — replacing weeks of traditional ground survey work. Aerial surveys are increasingly required for SETENA environmental pre-feasibility studies, municipal building permits, and construction financing due diligence. See our full guide to drone surveys for Costa Rica real estate.

Construction Monitoring & Earthwork

Active construction sites benefit from monthly or bi-weekly aerial surveys that track earthwork volumes, verify cut-and-fill quantities, and document progress for lenders and project managers. Comparing sequential aerial surveys generates precise volume change calculations — critical for contractor payment verification and schedule tracking. Our construction drone survey guide covers the full workflow.

Agriculture & Precision Farming

Pineapple plantations, palm oil estates, sugarcane fields, and specialty crop operations across Costa Rica use multispectral and RGB aerial surveys to map crop vigor, identify stressed zones, detect irrigation irregularities, and plan input applications. The NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) maps produced by aerial photogrammetry enable zone-specific treatment that reduces costs while increasing yields.

Environmental Impact Assessments (SETENA)

SETENA environmental permits require detailed topographic and land-use mapping. Aerial surveys generate the high-resolution orthophotos and DTMs that environmental consultants need to classify terrain, map vegetation, identify wetlands and riparian zones, and assess project impact. Drone-derived data is accepted by SETENA and reduces consultant time significantly compared to fieldwork-only methods. See our drone survey guide for EIA projects.

Topographic Mapping for Permits & Plans

The CFIA (Cólegio Federado de Ingenieros y de Arquitectos) requires certified topographic surveys for building permits, road projects, and subdivision plans. Drone aerial surveys provide the base topographic data — elevation contours, drainage channels, and terrain slope — that licensed topógrafos use to prepare their certified planos. Read our guide to drone topographic surveys in Costa Rica for more detail.

Aerial Survey Accuracy: What to Expect

Professional drone aerial surveys in Costa Rica achieve the following accuracy levels:

Photogrammetry (RTK) ±2–5 cm ±3–8 cm Open terrain, real estate, agriculture
LiDAR (DJI L1) ±1–3 cm ±1–3 cm Forested land, construction, high-precision
LiDAR + Ground Control ±1–2 cm ±1–2 cm Cadastral, legal surveys, volume calculations

All surveys are delivered with a documented accuracy report showing root-mean-square error (RMSE) at independent check points. This is provided as standard — not on request — because every deliverable should be verifiable. What Do You Receive? Aerial Survey Deliverables

Depending on the sensor used and the scope agreed, a professional aerial survey in Costa Rica delivers:

  • Georeferenced Orthophoto — High-resolution aerial image corrected for distortion and tied to real-world coordinates (CRTM05, WGS84, or your preferred system). Typical resolution: 2–5 cm/pixel.
  • Digital Terrain Model (DTM) — Bare-earth surface elevation model from LiDAR, or a DSM from photogrammetry for open terrain. Deliverable in GeoTIFF and point cloud formats.
  • Contour Lines — 0.5 m or 1 m interval elevation contours in DWG/DXF format, ready for import into AutoCAD, Civil 3D, or ArcGIS.
  • 3D Point Cloud — Full LAS/LAZ point cloud for BIM software, Revit, Civil 3D, or visualization platforms.
  • Volume Reports — Cut-and-fill calculations comparing current terrain to a design surface or prior survey epoch. Delivered as PDF and Excel.
  • 3D Mesh & Textured Model — Photorealistic 3D model for client presentations, planning reviews, and visualization.
  • Progress Photography — Nadir and oblique aerial photographs for reports, lender packages, and project archives.
  • Accuracy Report — Documented RMSE at check points, coordinate system metadata, and QC summary.

Deliverables are provided in formats compatible with AutoCAD, Civil 3D, ArcGIS, QGIS, Google Earth Pro, Procore, and Excel. If your team uses a specific platform, tell us before the survey and we will confirm compatibility.

DGAC Regulations for Aerial Surveys in Costa Rica

All commercial drone operations in Costa Rica are regulated by the DGAC (Dirección General de Aviación Civil). Operating without proper authorization exposes clients and operators to significant legal liability and can invalidate survey deliverables for official use.

As a licensed commercial operator, we handle all regulatory requirements as part of every project:

  • DGAC operational authorization — Maintained current for all commercial survey flights
  • Airspace clearance — We check controlled, restricted, and prohibited airspace for every survey location before mobilizing
  • Airport proximity requests — For sites within 9 km of Juan Santamaría, Liberia, Quepos, or other controlled airports, we obtain advance DGAC authorization (typically 3–5 business days)
  • National park compliance — SINAC permits obtained where required for surveys adjacent to or within protected areas

You do not need to do anything to obtain permits — we manage the full compliance process and confirm all authorizations before your survey date. For the full regulatory picture, see our guide to DGAC drone regulations in Costa Rica 2026.

Choosing the Right Aerial Survey Company in Costa Rica

Not all drone operators offer the same level of service. When selecting an aerial survey provider in Costa Rica, the key criteria are:

Licensed & DGAC-Authorized

Confirm that the operator holds current DGAC commercial drone operation authorization. Unlicensed operators expose you to legal liability and produce deliverables that may not be accepted by government agencies, CFIA engineers, or financial institutions.

Professional Survey-Grade Equipment

Survey accuracy requires RTK or PPK GPS correction and a calibrated sensor. Consumer-grade drones without RTK produce data suitable only for visual reference, not measurement or legal use. Professional aerial survey equipment includes: DJI Matrice 300 RTK, DJI Zenmuse L1 LiDAR, DJI Zenmuse P1 full-frame photogrammetry camera, and certified ground control equipment.

Deliverables You Can Actually Use

Aerial survey deliverables should be provided in standard GIS and CAD formats with documented accuracy reports. Orthophotos, DTMs, and contour files should be in the coordinate reference system your engineers use (typically CRTM05 in Costa Rica).

Transparent Pricing

Professional aerial survey pricing should be clear and based on documented criteria: site area, sensor type, and travel distance. Our online calculator provides instant itemized quotes with no hidden fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an aerial survey take?

The flight itself typically takes 30 minutes to 3 hours depending on site area. Data processing takes 24–48 hours. Most aerial surveys are completed and delivered within 72 hours of the flight date. Large or complex sites requiring LiDAR processing may take 3–5 days. Rush delivery (24-hour turnaround) is available for an additional fee.

Can aerial surveys be done in the rainy season?

Yes — Costa Rica’s rainy season runs May through November, but surveys are conducted year-round by scheduling flights during morning dry windows. Caribbean-side terrain is flyable in the dry months (December through April) but can be surveyed year-round with careful weather monitoring. We track forecasts and reschedule if weather conditions prevent safe and quality flight operations.

Is drone-derived data accepted for official permits in Costa Rica?

Yes — aerial survey data produced by licensed operators with calibrated equipment is accepted by SETENA, CFIA engineers, municipal offices, and financial institutions. For cadastral legal surveys, the data must be certified by a licensed topógrafo; our drone data serves as the high-accuracy base from which they produce their certified plano. For construction permits, topographic surveys, and EIA documentation, drone-derived deliverables are standard.
