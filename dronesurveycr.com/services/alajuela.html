Drone Survey Services in Alajuela

LiDAR mapping, photogrammetry, and topographic surveys for Costa Rica's largest province — from airport approach zones to Arenal volcano and the northern plains

Why Alajuela Province Needs Professional Drone Surveys

Alajuela is Costa Rica's second-most populous province and its largest by area, spanning from the Central Valley floor through the northern volcanic cordillera to the lowland plains bordering Nicaragua. This geographic diversity creates survey challenges found nowhere else in the country — from navigating the most restricted airspace around Juan Santamaría International Airport to mapping remote wetlands in Caño Negro Wildlife Refuge near the northern border.

The province's southern anchor is the greater Alajuela metropolitan area, where rapid urbanization along the Route 1 corridor between Alajuela city and San Ramón is converting agricultural land to residential and commercial development at one of the highest rates in the country. Real estate developers, road engineers, and municipal planners need accurate topographic data for site assessment, infrastructure design, and permit applications across these transitioning landscapes. Drone surveys deliver this data faster than ground crews working through congested corridors and construction zones.

Any drone operation near Juan Santamaría International Airport — Costa Rica's primary international gateway — requires careful DGAC coordination. The airport's approach and departure corridors extend across much of the Alajuela canton, creating restricted airspace zones where unauthorized drone flights are prohibited. Our team has extensive experience navigating DGAC authorization procedures for flights within these controlled zones, ensuring that construction sites, real estate properties, and agricultural operations near the airport receive the aerial survey data they need without regulatory complications.

In the northern reaches of the province, Arenal Volcano and Lake Arenal anchor a tourism and hydroelectric economy that generates substantial demand for aerial surveys. Hotel developments, adventure tourism infrastructure, wind farm installations around Lake Arenal, and ICE hydroelectric facilities all require topographic mapping. Further north, the agricultural plains around Los Chiles and Upala produce sugarcane, cattle, and tropical crops on flat terrain where drone photogrammetry efficiently covers large areas. Our DGAC-licensed pilots cover all sixteen cantons of Alajuela province, from airport-adjacent urban sites to the most remote northern borderlands.

Frequently Asked Questions — Drone Surveys in Alajuela

Can you fly drones near Juan Santamaría Airport?

Yes. We hold DGAC licenses and have established procedures for securing flight authorizations within the airport's controlled airspace. The authorization process typically takes 5-10 business days depending on the specific location relative to the runway. We handle all paperwork and coordination so your project proceeds without regulatory delays.

How much does a drone survey cost in Alajuela?

LiDAR surveys start at $1,000 base fee plus $80 per hectare. Projects near the airport may include additional coordination fees for DGAC authorization processing. Northern Alajuela sites in San Carlos or Los Chiles include mobilization costs reflecting the travel distance. A typical 5-hectare Central Valley site runs approximately $1,400. Contact us for a precise quote.

Do you survey around Arenal Volcano and Lake Arenal?

Yes. We regularly operate in the Arenal-La Fortuna area for tourism development, hydroelectric infrastructure, and conservation projects. We coordinate with DGAC for flights near Arenal Volcano and the La Fortuna airstrip. LiDAR is particularly valuable on Arenal's densely vegetated slopes where conventional aerial imaging cannot penetrate the canopy.

Can you survey large agricultural areas in northern Alajuela?

Yes. The flat terrain of the northern plains is ideal for efficient drone photogrammetry campaigns. We routinely cover properties of 50 to 500 hectares in the Los Chiles, Upala, and San Carlos districts for sugarcane operations, cattle ranches, and agro-industrial clients. Volume pricing makes large-area surveys cost-effective.

Do you cover all of Alajuela province?

Yes. We serve all sixteen cantons of Alajuela — from the airport-adjacent urban core through the Grecia-Sarchí highlands and San Ramón university town, north to Arenal and La Fortuna, and out to the border areas of Los Chiles, Upala, and Guatuso including Caño Negro Wildlife Refuge.
