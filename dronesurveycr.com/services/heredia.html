Drone Survey Services in Heredia

LiDAR mapping, photogrammetry, and topographic surveys for Costa Rica's coffee country — from Barva volcano slopes to Central Valley free trade zones

Why Heredia Province Needs Professional Drone Surveys

Heredia province occupies a strategic corridor in Costa Rica's Central Valley, climbing from the densely populated suburban floor at 1,100 meters elevation up the slopes of Barva Volcano to over 2,900 meters. Despite being the country's smallest province by area, Heredia packs extraordinary economic density — it hosts Costa Rica's premier coffee-growing highlands, its largest concentration of multinational technology companies, and some of the fastest-growing suburban municipalities in the metropolitan area.

The province's lower cantons — Santo Domingo, San Pablo, San Isidro, and Flores — have experienced rapid suburban expansion over the past two decades. Agricultural land that once grew coffee and sugarcane is being converted to residential condominiums, commercial plazas, and mixed-use developments at an accelerating pace. Accurate topographic data is essential for site planning, stormwater management, and municipal permitting in these transitioning landscapes where lot sizes are shrinking and density is increasing.

Heredia's free trade zones represent another major source of survey demand. The Ultra Park, Global Park, and Forum complexes that house operations for HP, Amazon, and the legacy Intel campus require regular topographic surveys for facility expansion, parking structure planning, and campus infrastructure upgrades. These corporate clients expect the fast turnaround and high accuracy that drone-based LiDAR delivers compared to traditional ground survey methods.

Above the suburban belt, Heredia's coffee-growing highlands around Barva, Santa Bárbara, San Rafael, and San Isidro de Heredia produce some of Costa Rica's most valued specialty coffees. Café Britt and Starbucks-contracted farms operate on steep volcanic slopes where terrain mapping is critical for erosion control, road maintenance, and shade-cover management. The province also borders Braulio Carrillo National Park, where any development requires environmental baseline surveys for SETENA permitting. Our DGAC-licensed operations cover all ten cantons of Heredia province.

Frequently Asked Questions — Drone Surveys in Heredia

Can you survey steep coffee farms on Barva volcano?

Yes. Our DJI Matrice 300 RTK operates effectively on the steep volcanic slopes that characterize Heredia's coffee highlands. LiDAR is particularly valuable here because it penetrates shade-tree canopy and coffee plant cover to map the actual ground surface, which is essential for erosion control and terrace design on gradients that often exceed 30 degrees.

How much does a drone survey cost in Heredia?

LiDAR surveys start at $1,000 base fee plus $80 per hectare. Heredia's proximity to our Central Valley base means lower mobilization costs for most projects. A typical 3-hectare suburban development site survey runs approximately $1,240. Contact us for a precise quote based on your project scope.

Do you work with free trade zone companies?

Yes. We have experience surveying corporate campus properties in Heredia's technology corridors. We coordinate with facility management and security teams, handle DGAC airspace authorizations, and deliver data in the CAD and GIS formats that engineering firms working on corporate expansion projects require.

Can you fly near Braulio Carrillo National Park?

Yes, with proper authorization. We coordinate with SINAC and DGAC for flights in national park buffer zones. Our surveys provide the environmental baseline data that SETENA requires for development permits near protected areas. We do not fly within national park boundaries without explicit SINAC research authorization.

Do you cover all of Heredia province?

Yes. We serve all ten cantons of Heredia — from the urban core and suburban belt of Heredia, Santo Domingo, San Pablo, and Flores through the coffee highlands of Barva, Santa Bárbara, and San Rafael to the northern lowlands of Sarapiquí, Puerto Viejo de Sarapiquí, and La Virgen.
