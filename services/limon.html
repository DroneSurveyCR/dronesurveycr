LiDAR mapping, photogrammetry, and aerial surveys for Costa Rica's Caribbean lowlands — from Tortuguero canals to Moín container port

Why Limón Province Needs Professional Drone Surveys

Limón province stretches along Costa Rica's entire Caribbean coast, from the Nicaraguan border south to the Panamanian frontier. This is the country's wettest region, receiving over 3,500 millimeters of rainfall annually in many areas, and its landscape is defined by dense tropical rainforest, extensive canal systems, flood-prone river basins, and a coastline shaped by constant Caribbean weather. Traditional ground surveying here is slow, expensive, and often physically impossible in the wet season.

The province's economy revolves around large-scale agriculture and port logistics. Banana and pineapple plantations operated by Dole, Chiquita, and Del Monte cover thousands of hectares across the Matina, Siquirres, and Guácimo lowlands. The Moín container terminal operated by APM Terminals is Central America's most modern port facility. EARTH University runs one of the tropics' largest agricultural research campuses near Guácimo. Each of these operations generates demand for precise aerial data — from plantation drainage mapping to port infrastructure monitoring.

Limón's terrain presents unique surveying challenges that make LiDAR indispensable. The province's dense rainforest canopy — particularly in the Tortuguero lowlands, Braulio Carrillo buffer zones, and the Talamanca indigenous territories — blocks conventional photogrammetry from reaching the ground surface. LiDAR's ability to penetrate multiple vegetation layers is critical for topographic surveys, flood modeling, and environmental impact assessments required by SETENA for development projects in this ecologically sensitive region.

The Río Reventazón, Río Pacuare, and Río Banano watersheds are among Costa Rica's most flood-prone river systems. After decades of catastrophic flooding events, municipalities and infrastructure agencies need accurate elevation data for flood risk modeling, drainage design, and disaster mitigation planning. Our DJI Matrice 300 RTK platform and DGAC-licensed pilots deliver this data efficiently across all six cantons of Limón province.

Frequently Asked Questions — Drone Surveys in Limón

Can drones operate in Limón's heavy rainfall conditions?

Yes. We monitor weather windows carefully and schedule flights during dry periods, which occur even in Limón's wet climate. Our DJI Matrice 300 RTK has IP45 weather resistance. For large plantation projects, we plan multi-day campaigns to work around afternoon rain patterns typical of the Caribbean lowlands.

Can LiDAR see through Limón's dense rainforest canopy?

Yes, and this is precisely why LiDAR is essential for Limón province. The laser pulses penetrate multiple vegetation layers — canopy, understory, and ground cover — to generate accurate ground-level elevation models. In the Tortuguero lowlands and Talamanca foothills, LiDAR is often the only practical method for obtaining topographic data.

How much does a drone survey cost in Limón?

LiDAR surveys start at $1,000 base fee plus $80 per hectare. Limón projects may include additional mobilization costs depending on site location. Large plantation surveys covering hundreds of hectares benefit from volume pricing. Contact us for a project-specific quote.

Do you provide surveys for SETENA environmental permits?

Yes. We deliver the topographic and vegetation mapping data that environmental consultants need for Environmental Impact Assessment reports submitted to SETENA. Our LiDAR data meets the accuracy standards required for permit applications in protected zones, coastal setback areas, and wetland buffer zones throughout Limón province.

Do you cover the entire Limón province?

Yes. We serve all six cantons — Limón, Pococí, Siquirres, Talamanca, Matina, and Guácimo. From the Moín port facilities and EARTH University campus to the remote Tortuguero canals and the Cahuita-Puerto Viejo tourism corridor, we operate throughout the province.
